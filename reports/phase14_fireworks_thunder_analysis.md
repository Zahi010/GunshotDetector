## 1. Executive Summary

Forensic audits in Phase 12 and Phase 13 established that **fireworks and thunderstorm claps account for over 98% of all continuous false alerts** generated by the detector. 

Phase 14 conducted an isolated, empirical acoustic investigation of all available fireworks ($N=20$) and thunderstorm recordings ($N=60$) to determine:
1. Which acoustic, spectral, and temporal features genuinely distinguish ballistic events from pyrotechnic and atmospheric mimics.
2. Why crest-factor filtering alone fails to suppress fireworks false positives.
3. Why naive semantic dominance suppression severely damaged genuine gunshot recall in Phase 13.
4. How calibrated semantic gating and multi-window confirmation decouple mimics from true ballistic events.

## 3. Fireworks Investigation: The Acoustic Ambiguity Dilemma

### Why Fireworks Confuse Neural Classifiers
Both fireworks (aerial shells, firecrackers, ground salutes) and gunshots are **rapid pyrotechnic deflagrations or detonations** that create expanding acoustic shock fronts in ambient air.
- Both produce high peak-to-RMS ratios (crest factor > 20 dB).
- Both produce high initial raw ensemble scores ($\ge 0.75$ in 34 cycle chunks).
- YAMNet's training representations (from AudioSet YouTube videos) contain high label noise between fireworks and gunshots, causing Google's pre-trained backbone to activate the `Fireworks` class (scores $\sim 0.26\text{--}0.51$) on real AR-15 and 12-gauge shotgun audio.

### Rejection vs. Accidental Suppression Trade-off
In Phase 13, the rule:
$$\text{If } S_{\text{fireworks}} > 0.25 \land S_{\text{fireworks}} \ge 1.5 \times S_{\text{gunshot}} \implies S_{\text{calib}} = S_{\text{raw}} \times 0.35$$
reduced continuous fireworks alerts from 165 to 113 (-31.5%). However, it accidentally suppressed **5 genuine gunshot events** in the development benchmark:
- `mitchell_mossberg_12g_08` ($S_{\text{fw}} = 0.301, S_{\text{gun}} = 0.175$)
- `mitchell_colt_ar15_01` ($S_{\text{fw}} = 0.261, S_{\text{gun}} = 0.125$)
- `mitchell_colt_ar15_04` ($S_{\text{fw}} = 0.508, S_{\text{gun}} = 0.070$)
- `mitchell_colt_ar15_05` ($S_{\text{fw}} = 0.302, S_{\text{gun}} = 0.117$)
- `p12_pos_005` ($S_{\text{fw}} = 0.349, S_{\text{gun}} = 0.156$)

### The Phase 14 Solution
Phase 14 decoupled fireworks suppression from genuine gunshots by enforcing an explicit **Ballistic Floor Safeguard**:
$$\text{Suppress Fireworks} \iff (S_{\text{fireworks}} > 0.35) \land (S_{\text{fireworks}} \ge 2.0 \times S_{\text{gunshot}}) \land (S_{\text{gunshot}} < 0.05)$$

**Outcome**:
- Accidental gunshot suppression: **0 events (100% genuine gunshot recall preserved)**.
- Continuous fireworks alerts: **152 alerts** (down from 165 in baseline).
- Demonstrates that further aggressive suppression of fireworks using YAMNet embeddings without auxiliary spatial sensor arrays inevitably harms life-safety ballistic detection.

## 4. Thunder Investigation: The Low-Frequency Acoustic Signature

Unlike fireworks, thunderstorm claps and rolling atmospheric thunder exhibit distinct physical properties that allow robust separation from gunshots:
1. **Extreme Sub-Bass Energy**: Welch PSD analysis reveals that **55.8%** of total acoustic energy in thunder recordings is concentrated below 300 Hz (compared to 18.5% in gunshots).
2. **Slow Acoustic Envelope Decay**: Thunder exhibits multi-second reverberation tails ($> 2.0\text{s}$) with low crest factor (mean: 16.59 dB vs >25 dB for fireworks).
3. **Semantic Distinction**: YAMNet's `Thunderstorm` and `Thunder` classes do not activate significantly on gunshot recordings ($S_{\text{thunder}} < 0.005$ on all 66 ballistic events).

### Suppression Performance for Thunder
Using relative semantic gating:
$$\text{If } S_{\text{thunder}} > 0.25 \land S_{\text{thunder}} \ge 1.5 \times S_{\text{gunshot}} \implies S_{\text{calib}} = S_{\text{raw}} \times 0.30$$
combined with single-window crest guarding ($\ge 12.0\text{ dB}$):
- Continuous thunder alerts dropped from **25 alerts** (Phase 9 baseline) to **13 alerts** (Phase 14 candidate) — a **48.0% reduction**.
- Accidental gunshot suppression: **0 events (0.0% collateral damage)**.

## 6. Engineering Conclusions

1. **Crest Factor Inefficacy for Pyrotechnics**: High crest factor is a necessary condition for impulsive events, but **insufficient** to distinguish gunshots from fireworks. Fireworks produce acoustic crest factors up to 37 dB.
2. **Thunder is Separable; Fireworks are Ambiguous**: Thunderclaps can be reliably filtered using low-frequency spectral energy and semantic suppression without harming gunshots. In contrast, mono-channel audio without direction-of-arrival or infrasound cannot completely eliminate fireworks false alerts without sacrificing rifle and shotgun detection.
3. **Operational Recommendation**: In urban deployments subject to holiday fireworks, operators should switch to the **`high_precision` operating profile** (`threshold_base = 0.80`, `cooldown = 2.5s`), which reduces continuous alerts to 71.5/hr (-25.9%) while accepting a controlled reduction in distant gunshot sensitivity.
