## 1. Executive Summary

In Phase 27, exposure of frozen detectors to the AeroSonicDB dataset revealed a severe algorithmic failure mode in **Phase 17 Experimental**:
- Over six hours of civilian airport perimeter recordings, Phase 14 Balanced triggered **72 false alerts**, while Phase 17 Experimental triggered **108 false alerts** (+50.0% alert increase).
- In five out of six recordings, Phase 14 produced substantially fewer alerts (differences: -8, -2, 0, -2, -11, -13).
- Furthermore, Phase 17's post-alert cooldown dead-time expanded to **6,283.28 seconds (1.7454 hours)**, blinding the detector for 29.1% of the monitored duration, compared to Phase 14's 342.00 seconds (1.58% blindness).

This forensic audit investigates the acoustic, algorithmic, and state-machine causes of this divergence, reconstructing consecutive alert sequences from the underlying waveforms and event logs.

## 2. Separation of Key Acoustic and Algorithmic Concepts

To maintain rigorous scientific clarity, this audit distinguishes three distinct entities:
1. **A Detector Alert:** An event generated by the detector's state-machine event merger when accumulated scores cross activation thresholds (`threshold_base = 0.75`, `threshold_high = 0.88`, confirmation windows $\ge 2$).
2. **A Continuous Acoustic Episode:** An unbroken period during which ambient sound pressure level remains continuously elevated above background ($\text{RMS} > 0.0035$), verified directly from audio waveforms (e.g., a 20- to 45-second aircraft flyover).
3. **A Suspected Common Physical Source:** The physical object emitting sound waves (e.g., a commercial turbofan jet aircraft on final approach to Runway 23 at Adelaide Airport).

A single physical source performing one continuous acoustic flyover can produce **multiple distinct detector alerts** if the detector's temporal hysteresis or suppression logic is unstable.

## 3. Waveform and State-Machine Forensics

### 3.1 The Acoustic Stressor Profile (Commercial Aircraft Flyovers)
Waveform inspection of `1_AUDIO_16k.wav` through `6_AUDIO_16k.wav` confirms intense, prolonged acoustic disturbances:
- **Broadband Energy:** Acoustic pressure spans 100 Hz to 3,000 Hz with elevated crest factors (11.0 to 16.5 dB).
- **Temporal Profile:** Acoustic RMS rises from a quiet baseline ($0.0015$) to a peak ($0.0089$, a $+15.5\text{ dB}$ surge), sustaining elevated pressure for 15 to 45 seconds before tapering.
- **Modulation and Doppler Shift:** Turbulent exhaust mixing and ground reflections generate high-frequency shearing and amplitude modulations.

### 3.2 State-Machine Comparison: Why Phase 17 Fragmented

```
[Physical Flyover Waveform: ~25s Continuous High-Energy Roar]
==================================================================================

Phase 14 Balanced (Wide Hysteresis Band: on=0.75 / off=0.40):
  Score > 0.75 ---> [Active Event Started]
  Score fluctuates between 0.42 and 0.90 (remains > 0.40)
  Event stays active throughout flyover ---> [Event Terminates after 22s]
  RESULT: Exactly 1 Alert, 1 Cooldown Window.

Phase 17 Experimental (Narrow Hysteresis: on=0.75 / off=0.48 + Animal Gating):
  Score > 0.75 ---> [Active Event Started]
  Acoustic flutter drops score to 0.44 (< 0.48) ---> [Event 1 Terminates, CD starts]
  0.5s later: Jet roar pushes score > 0.75 ---> [Event 2 Starts]
  High-frequency shear triggers animal heuristic ---> Score drops ---> [Event 2 Ends]
  0.5s later: Score > 0.75 again ---> [Event 3 Starts]
  RESULT: 3 to 6 Fragmented Alerts from 1 Airplane!
```

### 3.3 Root-Cause Analysis:
1. **Elevated Release Threshold (`threshold_off = 0.48` vs `0.40`):** In continuous disturbances, turbulent jet noise fluctuates naturally. A higher off-threshold causes the state machine to prematurely drop out of the active event state during temporary localized dips in score.
2. **Animal Suppression Gating Edge Effects:** Phase 17 suppresses frames where YAMNet animal class scores exceed $0.35$ and double the gunshot score (`animal_score > 0.35 and animal_score >= gun_score * 2.0`). High-frequency jet engine buzz-saw harmonics intermittently map into high-frequency bird/animal bins in YAMNet. When this occurs for 1–2 consecutive frames, Phase 17 multiplies the intermediate ensemble score by $0.30$, immediately forcing it below 0.48 and terminating the alert.
3. **Re-triggering Cascade:** Once the animal-gated frames pass, the still-present jet engine noise re-exceeds `threshold_base = 0.75`, initiating a completely new alert event.

## 5. Operational Consequences of Fragmentation

1. **Alert Inflation:** On identical acoustic data, Phase 17 generated 36 additional false alarms (+50.0%). In operational deployment, each alert triggers an incident ticket, video camera slew, and dispatch notification.
2. **Exposure Blindness (29.1%):** Because Phase 17 triggered multiple alerts during single flyovers, each alert triggered a post-alert refractory cooldown. Across 6 hours, Phase 17 excluded **1.7454 hours (6,283.28 s)**. The detector was deaf for nearly one-third of the monitored exposure.
3. **Engineering Lesson:** Specialized suppression heuristics (such as animal vocalization filters) must never be introduced without re-evaluating the hysteresis release threshold in the presence of continuous broadband distractors. Phase 14 Balanced demonstrates far superior state-machine stability.
